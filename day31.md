# 🎯 Objective

The objective of Day 31 was to build an interactive **AI Supply Chain Control Tower** using Claude AI.

The project simulates the experience of working as the **Head of Operations** in a global supply chain company.

The main goal was to monitor incoming operational alerts, prioritize incidents based on urgency, select corrective actions, and observe how each decision impacts important business KPIs.

This project helped me understand how real-time operational decision-making can be represented through an interactive simulation.

# 2️⃣ Project Overview

**AI Supply Chain Control Tower** is an interactive operations simulation that places the user in the role of a **Head of Operations**.

During the simulation, operational alerts continuously appear.

The player must:

* Identify critical incidents.
* Prioritize the most urgent problems.
* Select the best corrective action.
* Manage multiple incidents simultaneously.
* Balance cost and operational performance.
* Protect customer satisfaction and revenue.

The objective is to maximize operational performance before the simulation timer reaches zero.

---

# 3️⃣ Real-Time KPI Dashboard

The application includes a live KPI dashboard that tracks:

### Service Level

Measures the organization's ability to fulfill customer orders and maintain reliable service.

### Customer Satisfaction

Shows how operational decisions affect the customer experience.

### Inventory Health

Measures the availability and balance of inventory across the supply chain.

### Transportation Efficiency

Tracks the effectiveness of transportation and logistics operations.

### Operating Cost

Shows the financial impact of operational decisions.

### Revenue Protected

Measures the estimated business value protected through effective crisis management.

### Score

Represents the player's overall operational performance.

### Remaining Time

Displays the time remaining to manage incoming operational incidents.

# 4️⃣ Operational Alert System

The simulator randomly generates operational alerts.

Examples include:

1. Port Congestion
2. Supplier Delay
3. Truck Breakdown
4. Warehouse Running Out of Stock
5. Customs Inspection
6. Demand Spike
7. Factory Machine Failure
8. Weather Disruption
9. Wrong Inventory Count
10. Damaged Shipment

Each alert contains:

* Alert Title
* Short Description
* Priority Level
* Time Remaining
* Business Impact

Critical alerts are visually highlighted to help the player identify urgent problems quickly.

---

# 5️⃣ Incident Prioritization

One of the main challenges of the simulator is deciding which incident should be handled first.

The player must consider:

* Urgency
* Business Impact
* Customer Impact
* Financial Impact
* Time Sensitivity
* Future Risk

This demonstrates an important real-world operations concept:

**Not every problem can be solved at the same time, so effective prioritization is critical.**

---

# 6️⃣ Corrective Actions

Each operational alert provides different corrective actions.

Possible actions include:

1. Expedite Shipment
2. Use Backup Supplier
3. Reroute Trucks
4. Increase Production
5. Transfer Inventory
6. Approve Air Freight
7. Ignore
8. Delay Decision

Every action has different consequences.

Good decisions can:

* Improve KPIs
* Increase the operational score
* Reduce future risks
* Protect customer satisfaction
* Protect revenue

Poor decisions can:

* Reduce KPIs
* Increase operating costs
* Lower customer satisfaction
* Increase future operational risks

Some decisions may also create delayed consequences.

---

# 7️⃣ Increasing Difficulty

The simulation runs for **3 minutes**.

As the simulation progresses:

1. More alerts appear.
2. Alert frequency increases.
3. Multiple alerts remain active simultaneously.
4. The player must make decisions faster.
5. Prioritization becomes more important.

This creates a realistic pressure environment where operational leaders must make decisions under time constraints.
